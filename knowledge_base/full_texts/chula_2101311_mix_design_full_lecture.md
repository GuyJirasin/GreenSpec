## Page 3

Mix Design
Definition:
The proportioning of concrete mixtures
→ Process of determining the right combination of cement, aggregates, 
water, and admixtures
→ To make concrete that meets given specifications
Purpose:
•
To achieve required workability, strength, and durability
•
To obtain optimum performance at minimum cost
3

## Page 4

Importance of Workability
•
Workability directly affects pumpability and constructability
•
Poor workability → higher handling cost, low strength, and reduced 
durability
•
For high-consistency concrete → use water-reducing or set-
retarding admixtures instead of adding water at site
Concrete properties can be oppositely affected by one variable
Example:
•
Adding extra water → improves flowability → but reduces strength
•
Always balance between workability and strength

## Page 7

Routine Mix Design Practice
•
Usually, only workability and strength are specified
•
Durability often ignored — except for special environmental 
exposure
•
When durability or strength demands a lower W/C, this can be 
achieved by:
• Optimize aggregate grading
• Using water-reducing admixtures

## Page 8

Aggregate Grading and ASTM C33
•
It is often assumed that the densest aggregate packing with minimum 
voids is the most economical,
→ because it requires the least amount of cement paste to fill the voids.
•
ASTM C33 – the most widely used standard for aggregate grading.
•
Aggregates outside ASTM C33 limits often cause
→ workability problems and large voids in hardened concrete.
•
However, aggregates within ASTM C33 limits
→ may not always produce a satisfactory concrete mixture,
since performance also depends on particle shape, texture, and blending.

## Page 14

Procedures for mix design
•
The most popular method for mixture proportioning is recommended by 
ACI 211.1-91. → Strength-Based Mix Design 
•
There are 2 procedures
1. Weight method 
o Simpler and less exact
o Does not require specific gravity data of materials
2. Absolute volume method
o Proportions ingredients so that the sum of absolute volumes = 1 m³
o Considered more accurate and widely used
•
Both methods follow 9 basic steps, with the first 6 steps being common to 
both.

7.2 Absolute Volume Method
•
The required volume of fine aggregate is determined as:

Step 8: Adjustment for aggregate 
moisture
•
Aggregates are usually moist in 
stockpiles.
•
Without correction, the actual W/C ratio
in the trial mix will be higher than 
intended, and the aggregate weights will 
be lower than estimated.
•
Therefore, both aggregate weight and 
mixing water must be adjusted:
o Increase aggregate weights by the 
percentage of moisture content
(both absorbed and surface water).
o Reduce the mixing water by the 
amount of free moisture contributed 
by the aggregates (i.e., surface water 
= total moisture − absorption).
